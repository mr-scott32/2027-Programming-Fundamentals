# WAgile?

#### **Understanding WAgile as a Hybrid Model**

WAgile is a hybrid software development approach that combines elements of the traditional Waterfall model with Agile methodologies. It recognises that some projects require the structured planning and documentation associated with Waterfall, while also benefiting from the flexibility and responsiveness of Agile practices. Typically, early stages such as requirements gathering and high-level system design follow a structured, sequential process. Once these foundations are established, development and testing proceed in iterative cycles similar to Agile sprints. This blended structure allows teams to maintain control over scope and compliance requirements while still adapting to feedback and change.

#### **When and How Intervention is Applied During the Development Life Cycle**

In a WAgile model, intervention occurs at strategic points throughout the development life cycle. The “when” refers to predefined checkpoints, often after major phases such as requirements analysis or system design, where progress is reviewed and validated before moving forward. The “how” involves integrating Agile-style iterations within these broader structured stages. For example, once requirements are approved, development may occur in short cycles with continuous testing and stakeholder feedback. If significant issues arise, the project can return to structured planning before continuing. This approach ensures flexibility without sacrificing governance, documentation, or risk management.

#### **Scale and Types of Developments**

WAgile is most suitable for medium to large-scale developments where both control and adaptability are required. It is particularly effective in projects that operate in regulated industries, enterprise environments, or government sectors where documentation and compliance are essential, but user needs may still evolve during development. Projects involving complex systems, multiple stakeholders, or long timelines often benefit from the balance WAgile provides. Smaller, rapidly changing projects may favour pure Agile, while highly rigid, compliance-heavy projects may still prefer traditional Waterfall.

#### **Example**

A hospital developing a new patient management system would benefit from a WAgile approach. The project would begin with structured requirement analysis to ensure compliance with healthcare regulations, privacy laws, and data security standards. Once these core requirements are formally approved, development could proceed in iterative cycles, allowing medical staff to test modules such as appointment scheduling or electronic records and provide feedback. If regulatory updates or workflow changes occur, structured review points allow adjustments without disrupting the entire project. In this case, WAgile provides both the governance required for healthcare compliance and the flexibility needed to meet user needs effectively.
